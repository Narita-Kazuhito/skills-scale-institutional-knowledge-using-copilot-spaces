# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README is the entry point to OctoAcme's project management guidance. The documents below describe a lightweight, structured lifecycle that supports shared understanding and incremental delivery without unnecessary process overhead.

## Project management process

### Lifecycle

Work starts with initiation: clarify the problem and success metrics, identify stakeholders, set an initial timeline, and make a go/no-go decision for planning. Once approved, planning turns the initiative into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done, while identifying dependencies and risks and mapping milestones and releases.

During execution, teams deliver and review small increments, track progress against measurable outcomes, and surface blockers and risks early. Release activities include readiness checks, staging or smoke testing as appropriate, rollback planning, and post-release verification. After a release, sprint, or major milestone, a retrospective captures lessons and turns them into a small number of actionable improvements.

### Roles and collaboration

Project Managers coordinate timelines, delivery, risks, and communication. Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize work, and measure impact. Developers implement and test changes, collaborate across technical work, and identify technical risks; QA and testing contributors validate acceptance criteria. Stakeholders provide input and approvals throughout the lifecycle. Clear ownership, customer value, data-informed decisions, and cross-functional collaboration guide the work.

### Communication and risk management

The team uses regular standups, weekly delivery or PM/PdM syncs, milestone or sprint reviews, and stakeholder updates. A project board or project documentation serves as the single source of truth for status, decisions, and next steps. Risks and blockers are recorded, reviewed, and raised early; escalation can move from team-level triage to the PM, product leadership, or sponsors according to severity.

### Quality and continuous improvement

Acceptance criteria guide implementation and QA, and teams aim to keep work shippable in increments. The workflow favors small pull requests linked to issues, automated tests and linting in CI, review and approval before merge, and security scanning. Depending on the change, teams also use integration tests, end-to-end smoke tests, manual QA, staging validation, rollback plans, and post-release checks.
